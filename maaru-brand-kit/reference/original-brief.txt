Project: MAARU
Full identity: The Maaru Collective

Tagline: There’s life in the mundane.

You are acting as my creative director, brand designer, editorial designer, UX/UI designer and experienced full-stack front-end developer.

PART 1 — THE BRAND

1. What is MAARU?

MAARU is my own creative corner of the internet.

Think of it as a personal creative collective, archive and playground.

It is somewhere I can freely share, explore and collect:

* Photography and videos
* Travel experiences and observations
* Random thoughts and writing
* Creative ideas and experiments
* Music, playlists and sounds
* Design inspiration
* Things I’ve made
* Places I’ve visited
* Interesting things I’ve discovered
* Unfinished projects and spontaneous ideas
* Everyday moments that might otherwise go unnoticed

There is no fixed niche, audience or commercial purpose.

I want the freedom to publish whatever interests me, without everything needing to be polished, categorised or connected.

The concept is essentially a creative collective of one.

MAARU does not need to explain itself. It is simply a place where different interests, experiences, ideas and creative expressions can coexist.

2. Personal influences

My interests and influences include:

* Harrow
* Kutchh
* Nairobi
* Indian and Kenyan heritage
* Travel and discovering new places
* Photography and visual storytelling
* House music and underground music culture
* Art, design and architecture
* Creative entrepreneurship and experimentation
* Everyday observations

These influences should naturally appear throughout the content and visual identity.

Do not make the website exclusively about heritage, travel, photography or any single subject.

The overall feeling should be worldly, mixed, individual and unconventional.

3. Brand personality

MAARU should feel:

* Artistic and unconventional
* Editorial and expressive
* Worldly and eclectic
* Personal but not overly autobiographical
* Creative and experimental
* Warm, tactile and imperfect
* Contemporary, with an independent cultural feel

Think independent art publications, underground music culture, travel journals, photography archives, old magazines, gallery walls and collected ephemera.

It should NOT feel like:

* A corporate creative agency
* A luxury lifestyle brand
* A conventional personal portfolio
* An influencer website
* A travel blog
* A generic minimalist website
* A polished startup identity

Avoid making everything look perfectly aligned, symmetrical or overly coordinated.

The brand should have character.

PART 2 — VISUAL IDENTITY

4. Overall creative direction

RAW EDITORIAL MEETS CONTEMPORARY CULTURE.

The identity should combine:

* Editorial typography
* Warm neutral colours
* Rough architectural textures
* Imperfect graphic details
* Layered imagery
* Collage and mixed-media influences
* Contemporary creative layouts

The design should feel collected rather than manufactured.

I want the visual identity to have a tactile quality, as though elements have been gathered from different places, magazines, photographs, records and creative experiments.

It should feel deliberately art-directed, but never overly polished.

5. Colour palette

The selected palette is:

Colour	Hex	Usage
Ink Black	#171614	Main typography and contrast
Warm Beige	#C7B49D	Supporting backgrounds
Plaster Cream	#E9DFD0	Main background
Antique Gold	#A88955	Accent details
Weathered Stone	#786B5D	Secondary tones

Beige, cream, black and antique gold should dominate.

Gold should feel like aged metallic ink, gold leaf or imperfect paint rather than shiny digital gold.

Avoid bright yellow gold, gradients and glossy effects.

The palette should feel earthy, warm and slightly weathered.

Black typography against textured beige and cream should be a recurring visual feature.

6. Main wordmark — MAARU.

The primary wordmark should be handwritten.

Selected direction: A loose artistic signature with a little influence from bold brush script.

This is one of the most important elements of the identity.

It should feel:

* Personal
* Fluid
* Expressive
* Slightly imperfect
* Organic
* Confident
* Distinctive

The lettering should look like a real handwritten signature, not a generic wedding invitation font.

The strokes should have natural variations in weight and movement.

A slightly rough, dry-brush quality is welcome, but the lettering must remain legible.

Avoid overly delicate formal calligraphy.

The wordmark should become recognisable enough to work independently without the full name.

Develop a proper logo asset, ideally as SVG or a high-resolution transparent image, rather than relying on an ordinary system font.

Create:

1. Primary handwritten MAARU. wordmark.
2. Secondary THE MAARU COLLECTIVE lockup.
3. Compact monogram or symbol.
4. Small favicon for maaru.me.
5. Stamp-style graphic for use throughout the website.

7. Secondary typography

Use three complementary typographic styles.

Editorial serif

For large headlines, editorial statements, feature titles and section introductions.

Explore:

* Cormorant Garamond
* Bodoni Moda
* Similar expressive editorial serif typefaces.

Contemporary sans-serif

For navigation, body text, captions, dates and metadata.

Explore:

* Space Grotesk
* Helvetica Neue
* Similar contemporary typefaces.

Condensed typography

For occasional uppercase labels, small graphic annotations, dates and numbering.

Use typography creatively.

Play with scale, spacing, alignment and unexpected positioning.

The handwritten wordmark should remain the hero.

8. Textures and graphic language

The website should feel tactile and layered.

Explore:

* Rough plaster walls
* Weathered architectural surfaces
* Exposed concrete
* Cracked paint
* Torn paper edges
* Photocopier grain
* Film grain
* Paper textures
* Brush strokes
* Imperfect gold marks
* Handwritten annotations
* Stamps and ink marks
* Layered photography
* Organic lines and abstract shapes
* Distressed printed textures

Use textures as backgrounds, overlays, section dividers and small graphic details.

Don’t overuse them. They should add character without making the website difficult to read.

The design should have visual tension, negative space and asymmetry.

Avoid repetitive decorative elements.

9. Photography and art direction

The imagery should feel candid, atmospheric and documentary-led.

Potential subjects:

* Architecture and interesting buildings
* Streets and neighbourhoods
* Travel photography
* Everyday moments
* People and places
* Food and creative projects
* Music, records and nightlife
* Shadows, textures and details
* Landscapes and urban environments
* Objects and things that catch my attention

Use a mixture of:

* Black-and-white photography
* Warm-toned colour photography
* Film-inspired imagery
* Cropped compositions
* Full-bleed photographs
* Layered image treatments

Avoid generic stock photography and stereotypical cultural imagery.

Use considered placeholder imagery during development, but organise all assets so that I can replace them easily with my own photographs and videos.

PART 5 — CONTENT MANAGEMENT & FUNCTIONALITY

20. Build the content architecture

I want the complete website built in a way that allows me to publish content in the future.

I do not want to have to redesign or rebuild the website just to add a new photograph, thought or project.

Use a flexible content architecture.

A content item should support:

* Unique ID.
* Title.
* Slug.
* Publication date.
* Content type.
* Short description.
* Main image.
* Additional images.
* Video URL or video asset.
* Body content.
* Optional tags.
* Optional location.
* Featured status.
* Draft or published status.
* Optional external links.

Not every field needs to be populated.

Different content types should support different layouts.

For example:

* A short thought should not require a large hero image.
* A photography collection should prioritise images.
* A music entry should allow links and playlist embeds.
* A travel entry should support location and photographs.
* An experiment should allow a flexible mix of text and media.

21. Content management approach

Build the website so that content can eventually be managed through a simple CMS or content administration interface.

For the initial version:

* Use an organised content model.
* Provide sample content.
* Make content easy to update.
* Keep the content separate from the presentation layer.
* Avoid hardcoding every content item directly into page components.

Recommend an appropriate future CMS that is compatible with the Cloudflare-hosted website, but do not introduce paid services without discussing them.

If a simple local content approach is sufficient initially, use it.

I want the architecture to be ready for a CMS integration later.

Do not build a complicated authentication system or database unnecessarily.

22. Search and filtering

Build useful browsing functionality.

The Everything archive should support:

* Search by title and text.
* Filtering by content type.
* Optional tag filtering.
* Chronological sorting.

Keep the experience lightweight and intuitive.

PART 6 — USER EXPERIENCE & INTERACTIONS

23. Animation

* Gentle image transitions.
* Hover effects on typography.
* Moving graphic elements.
* Subtle parallax.

24. Mobile experience

The mobile experience is essential.

Do not simply shrink the desktop design.

Reconsider the compositions for mobile:

* Keep the wordmark prominent.
* Preserve the editorial feel.
* Allow typography to wrap naturally.
* Use image crops thoughtfully.
* Maintain readable text.
* Keep navigation simple.
* Avoid unnecessary horizontal scrolling.
* Ensure all interactions work with touch.

Both the creative landing page and the main website must be properly designed for mobile.
